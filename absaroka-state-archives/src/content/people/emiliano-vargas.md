## Background and beliefs
Vargas's supplied biography describes service with revolutionary forces associated with Pancho Villa. His movement promotes land reform, worker solidarity, opposition to concentrated wealth, and anti-imperialism. His slogan is **Tierra, Trabajo y Libertad** (“Land, Labor, and Liberty”). His exact birth date, family background and formal Mexican-government affiliation are unknown.

## Border conflict
Earlier League messaging claimed New Austin belonged to Mexico and warned of armed resistance to American expansion. A resolution-related statement said the League would “resist by force of arms,” in the context of opposing territorial acquisition. That is not evidence Vargas ordered attacks on civilians.

## Later statements
In a poster dated 1923, Vargas said his allegiance was to the people and denied seeking to conquer New Austin in Mexico's name. He welcomed Oak Woodson's proposal to meet with residents and officials. Whether he formally withdrew all earlier territorial claims is unresolved.

## Allegations
Claims of robberies or killings attributed to League members have circulated, but specific incidents and Vargas's personal responsibility have not been established in the supplied material.
